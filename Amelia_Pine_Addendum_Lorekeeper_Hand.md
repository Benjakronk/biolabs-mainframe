# Addendum — The Lorekeeper Hand & Amelia's Politics

**Companion to `Dr_Amelia_Pine_Character_Bible.md` and `What_Happened_To_Aurora.md`.**
*GM-facing. This addendum adds locked canon to both documents and supersedes any earlier framing of the trial overload as a pure accident. Where it touches reserved dials in the parent docs, it says so.*

---

## 1. What Actually Happened to Aurora — the Buried Cause

The parent doc treats the experience-sharing trial as a catastrophic accident: a channel that overloaded, a self that bled across the bond into P-1. That remains true at the level of mechanism. What changes is the cause behind the cause.

**The overload was sabotage.** The trial did not fail on its own. The **Lorekeepers** tampered with it deliberately, engineering the channel failure that emptied Aurora's mind into P-1.

Their motive has nothing to do with Amelia, Scepter, or the Share itself. **It is about Aurora's other work.** Aurora Pine was an archaeologist at Grandharbor Academy whose research circled **Temporis** — the wound in time, the entity the Lorekeepers guard most jealously of all. Her published bulletin already records, in her own academic hedging, that access to key sites was "repeatedly denied by unnamed authorities" and that the work met "considerable resistance from certain academic quarters." That resistance was the Lorekeepers. When denial of access stopped being enough — when Aurora kept assembling the pattern anyway — they moved from obstruction to erasure.

The experience-sharing trial was the instrument of opportunity. A Lorekeeper, or an Outer Circle associate acting on instruction without full knowledge of why, ensured the channel would fail catastrophically. The cover is perfect: a tragic accident in experimental bond-science, the kind of thing that happens at the bleeding edge. No one looks for a hand behind it because the technology is dangerous enough to fail honestly. Aurora's Temporis research died with her lucid mind. That was the point.

**This reframes the whole tragedy without softening Amelia's guilt.** She still told Aurora the trial was harmless. She still read her the flyer, still laughed about the money, still delivered her sister to the table. The penance is intact — sharper, even, because the thing she vouched for was a murder weapon she couldn't have known was loaded. The cruelty of her position deepens: she blames herself completely for a death she did not cause and could not have prevented, while the parties who *did* cause it remain invisible to her.

---

## 2. The Thing Amelia Does Not Know

**Amelia does not know the Lorekeepers exist.** This is the load-bearing constraint of the entire arc, and it must hold until the GM chooses to break it.

She has no name for them, no concept of an order, no suspicion that a hidden faction shaped her sister's fate. Everything she has built — Scepter membership, Proteus, the reconstruction, the whole grief-engine — runs on a false attribution. She believes the trial was an accident and that the institutions failing to help her now are the League and the Federation's research bureaucracy. She is, in the most literal sense, hunting in the wrong direction. Her brilliance is aimed at reversing the *mechanism* (recover the displaced self, rebuild Aurora) and never at the *cause* (who did this, and why), because she does not know there is a cause to find.

This produces a specific, usable dramatic irony. The player who has met Lorekeepers, or read the Temporis material, holds a piece of Amelia's story that Amelia herself does not. She has spent years and her own conscience chasing a cure for what she thinks was bad luck. The truth — that her sister was deliberately silenced for research the party may themselves be brushing against — is sitting one revelation away, and it is not hers yet.

**The reveal is a reserved beat, not a fact in play.** Do not let it leak through her dialogue, her notes, or her behavior. Her files circle the League and the trial's technical failure; they never circle a hidden order, because she cannot circle what she's never conceived of. If the party ever hands her this truth — that the Lorekeepers killed Aurora's mind to bury Temporis — it should land as the single most destabilizing thing anyone has ever told her. It would either redirect the entire engine (a new enemy, finally the right one) or break it (the cure was never the point; she was grieving the wrong accident this whole time). That choice belongs to the table.

## 3. Amelia's Actual Politics

The parent bible frames her Scepter membership as purely instrumental — access, not ideology. That stays the spine. This addendum adds texture without overturning it: **she is not an empty opportunist wearing the uniform. She is a sympathizer who would not have come on her own.**

**She is not Nero's.** Her core drive is Aurora, and it would remain Aurora if Scepter's larger vision evaporated tomorrow. She has no investment in bringing Nero's particular dream to life; the revolution is the building she happens to be standing in because that's where the equipment is. If a cure existed somewhere else, she would walk to it without a backward glance.

**But the grievance is real, and it predates her need.** Amelia harbors genuine resentment toward the **League** for what it has done — the suppression, the regulatory walls, the institutional indifference she ran into when she tried to get answers about Aurora through legitimate channels. The parent bible's `records-wall.txt` already shows this: medical records hitting walls, official explanations that explained nothing, doors that closed in her face. She experienced the League as a wall between herself and her sister's recovery, and she has not forgiven it. So when Scepter's rank-and-file talk about a corrupt League that hoards knowledge and crushes the people who need it, **she agrees.** She has lived the thesis. The diagnosis is correct; she simply joined for the lab, not the cause.

This is the cleanest possible expression of how Scepter actually recruits, per canon: through legitimate grievance, where the rank-and-file are right about the problems and wrong about the solution. Amelia is that pattern made personal. She is right that the League failed her. She is wrong about almost everything that follows from it — wrong about who really wronged her (she blames the League for a crime the Lorekeepers committed), wrong that Scepter is anything but another machine that will use her, wrong that any of this brings Aurora back. Her politics are sincere and entirely misdirected, which is the most tragic configuration available: a real wound, a real anger, both aimed at the wrong target by an enemy she'll never see.

**Practical register for the table.** When Amelia speaks of the League, let the bitterness be genuine and specific, never sloganeering. She doesn't recite Scepter talking points; she describes the night a clerk told her Aurora's file was sealed, the committee that wouldn't see her, the research she wasn't cleared to read. The ideology lives in the particulars of her own injury. And she never reaches past the League toward anyone hidden behind it — because for her, there is no one there.

---

## 4. Canon Status

**Locked:** the trial overload was deliberate Lorekeeper sabotage; the motive was burying Aurora's Temporis research; Amelia does not know the Lorekeepers exist and has not learned the truth of their interference; Amelia is genuinely sympathetic to Scepter's anti-League grievance while not being committed to Nero's vision; her resentment is aimed at the League, misattributing the Lorekeepers' crime.

**Reserved (GM's beat):** whether, when, and how Amelia ever learns the truth, and whether learning it redirects or shatters her. The Aurora-as-Temporis-researcher thread also gives the party an independent route to this truth that does not pass through Amelia at all — they may learn what was done to Aurora before Amelia does, or instead of her.

**Supersedes:** any reading in which the trial was a blameless accident. The mechanism was an accident's shape; the cause was a hand.
