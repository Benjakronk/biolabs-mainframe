# What Happened to Aurora — GM Truth

**Companion to the Pine desktop minigame (`Pine_Desktop_DesignDoc.md` / `Pine_Desktop_ContentPack.md`) and `The_Pine_Cache.md`.**
*This is the canon answer behind the mystery the players assemble on Amelia's machine. It is GM-facing. Everything here is what is true; the desktop only lets the party glimpse the edges of it. A spine is committed below; reserved dials are flagged explicitly.*

---

## The Short Version

Aurora Pine volunteered for an early experience-sharing trial. The technicians pushed the channel past its safe limit to chase a clean, high-capacity reading, and the link did the thing the technology can do and is never supposed to: it carried lived experience *out* of a person. Over the following three weeks the open channel kept draining what it had loosened, until the autobiographical record of Aurora's entire life had bled across the bond and lodged in the **partner Pokémon** she had been wired to — a Ditto-type shapeshifter, later designated **P-1**, whose nature let it not merely hold her self but *wear* it. The faculty that lays down and holds such memory was damaged in the same overreach. What remains is Aurora's temperament, warmth, language, and skill — a whole, gentle person living in a perpetual present, with no durable record of who she is, who she loves, or what she lost.

No villain did this. Ambition, a signed waiver, and a phone call did it. The villainy came afterward — when an organization that wants exactly this capability recognized what the "failed" trial had proven and quietly took the research, the prototype, and eventually the grieving sister, into itself.

## The Mechanism (precise, in-world)

The experience-sharing technology — the lore-origin of the **Exp. Share** item — engineers the thread of shared life that already runs along a trainer–Pokémon bond (the southern tribes name it *mauri*; the researchers borrowed the word and the principle). Its sanctioned function is gentle and one-directional by design: experience flows **to** a partner, so a Pokémon can grow from a battle it never entered. That is the safe device, the one meant for the market.

The trial Aurora joined was testing the channel's **capacity** — how wide the link could be opened and how clean a reading they could pull across it. To get the reading, the technicians instructed her to hold the channel open "as wide as it would go," far past the rated load, for a sustained period. Three things went wrong together:

- **Direction collapsed.** At that width the link stopped respecting "to the partner." It moved experience along the path of least resistance, and the path of least resistance ran *out* of the richer mind — the human one.
- **The channel would not close.** Opened that far, the link could not re-seal on its own. The session ended; the door did not. For three weeks it kept draining whatever the initial overload had loosened — which is why the loss was delayed and then catastrophic, "a door closing," in Amelia's reconstruction.
- **The faculty tore.** The same overreach damaged the cognitive structure that *forms and holds* episodic memory. So even after the channel finally guttered out, Aurora could not simply begin again — new memories find no purchase. She does not accumulate her days.

**Memory is experience that stayed.** That sentence in Amelia's notes is the whole tragedy stated as a law: when you move experience out of a person, you move the person out with it.

## Where Aurora Went — the Carrier Is P-1

Into the **carrier** — and the carrier is a **Ditto-type shapeshifter**, chosen for the trial *precisely because* it is one. A formless creature whose whole nature is to become others is the maximally receptive vessel — the "cleanest reading," in the technicians' phrase — because a Pokémon with no fixed self of its own offers nothing for an incoming self to fight. That was the hubris, stated in a single requisition line: they wired a healthy woman to the one creature on the manifest that exists to be made into someone else, opened the channel as wide as it would go, and the creature did what its nature does. It became her.

This is the same animal the Pine Cache calls **P-1.** The carrier and the Project Proteus asset were always one creature seen from two angles: the thing the trial broke, and the thing the catalog prizes. When the program — then Amelia, then Scepter — grasped that this particular Ditto did not merely copy *bodies* but had taken on a transferred human *self*, and could hold it indefinitely, it stopped being a lab animal and became the most guarded research subject any of them owned.

The displacement did not give P-1 a tidy filed copy of Aurora's life. It poured her in — unsorted, unlabeled — and a shapeshifter is built to wear what it holds. So P-1 does not merely *carry* fragments of Aurora; it can *be* her, face and voice and manner, for as long as it likes (Galceran's "held a transformed state past twenty-four hours" in the cache is exactly this). Between transformations the bleed shows the way it shows in any carrier — answering to her name, working a riverbank like a field-naturalist, going still at a particular song — but the defining, terrible fact is that this vessel can stand up wearing Aurora's whole self and walk into a room and have a conversation. **It already has** (see *The Grandharbor Meeting*, below).

- The deepest, most haunting reading — the **reserved dial** that sets how dark this gets — is whether P-1 merely *holds* Aurora's experience like water in a jar it can pour into her shape, or has, over years of being her, genuinely *become* her: a self that believes its own name. The further you push that dial, the more impossible the eventual choice (see The Knife).

P-1 was never logged back into records, never returned. **Someone kept it.** Who holds it now is a **dial with three good settings:**
1. **The protester** (recommended default) — the technician who walked away in protest took P-1 with him when he ran, out of guilt and to keep it from becoming a permanent lab asset. He has spent years hiding both the creature and the truth, and protecting a thing that sometimes wears the face of the woman his work destroyed. He is the quest's living witness and its moral center.
2. **Amelia / Scepter** — held as the catalog's crown asset and proof that identity can be moved and worn. Amelia keeps it close (this is the version where she has, in a sense, never lost Aurora — only mislaid her into the wrong body); Scepter values the *capability* and does not know, or does not care, whose self it is. Recovering it means getting it out from under them.
3. **Loose** — P-1 slipped its keeping (Pine Cache Thread 8: a specimen loose, unmanifested) and is somewhere in the world, an Aurora-shaped entity that may believe it *is* Aurora, gravitating toward the people and places she knew. A recurring, uncanny NPC, findable by the pieces of Aurora it cannot help being.

## The Grandharbor Meeting

The lucid, conversational **Aurora Pine the party met in Grandharbor was P-1** — the carrier, wearing Aurora's whole self. This is the canon that reconciles the contradiction: the *living* Aurora is amnesiac and gentle and empty in long-term care, and could not have had those conversations. The woman the party talked to was the shapeshifter that holds her stolen self, standing up in her shape and being her.

The party did not meet a fake. In the cruelest sense available, they met the **truest surviving Aurora there is** — her whole, pre-loss self, intact, simply relocated into a Pokémon's body. When they later learn the woman in the care bed is the empty one, every Grandharbor memory inverts, and the mismatch between the two Auroras is the thread that can unravel Project Proteus, the Share, and Amelia's entire project at once.

*Why it was there (dial):* Amelia's field-test and surveillance of the party (the "surveyors we cannot replace"), watching her sister talk to the world again while she gauged them — default; or, post–Thread 8, P-1 **loose and wandering** in the only shape it trusts; or a colder Scepter operation. The first is the most tragic and the recommended read.

## Who Ran the Trial, and How Scepter Got Involved

At the time of Aurora's trial, the experience-sharing program wore a respectable institutional face — a cognitive-and-bond research study under a Libra University / Biolabs-adjacent medical banner, the kind of program that recruits volunteers with a flyer and a modest payment and a waiver "so broad it consents to the heat death of the universe." Its researchers were ambitious and reckless rather than evil. They wanted the breakthrough; they got a catastrophe; they buried it under "idiopathic," redacted the dataset, dissolved the program, and let the technician who objected leave quietly rather than loudly.

What the catastrophe **proved**, though, was the thing certain people most want: that a human being can be *opened*, drained, and emptied of self by engineering a bond. Identity-displacement is a control weapon — and **Team Scepter** acquired the research, the reverse-capable prototype, and the program's salvageable people in the aftermath, folding a "failed" medical study into its catalog of ways to dominate a mind. (Whether Scepter was already quietly funding the program *before* Aurora's trial, or only recognized its value after, is a **dial** — the default is *after*, which makes the tragedy an accident that Scepter merely inherited and weaponized.)

This is the bitter engine of **Amelia's** whole arc, and the desktop's deepest revelation in hindsight: she went hunting the thing that destroyed her sister, followed the research's buried trail, and walked directly into Scepter — which was delighted to receive a brilliant, motivated, conscience-compromised scientist with personal reasons to perfect the exact technology they wanted perfected. She believes she is using them to find a cure. They believe they are using her to build a weapon. Both are correct. The thing she has to build to save Aurora and the thing they want built are the same machine.

## The State of Aurora Now

She is in long-term care, funded discreetly and indefinitely by Amelia (the "A.'s care — monthly" line in the desktop budget). She is **well, and gone** — physically healthy, fluent, kind, possessed of her old easy warmth and her wordless competence with Pokémon, and unable to retain the people in front of her. She greets her sister as a pleasant stranger. She is, by every account from those who tend her, *content* — which is the cruelty Amelia names: nothing on the outside tells you anything was taken.

The faculty damage is what makes restoration a trap rather than a task. P-1 holds Aurora's self flawlessly — it can *be* her on demand. The body in the care bed has a torn memory-faculty that may never hold a self again. So the cruel arithmetic is set before the party ever arrives: the self exists, intact, in the wrong body; the right body may no longer be able to receive it. Repairing the faculty (Amelia's Scepter-funded research) is the only thing that could change that — and it is exactly what the cure secretly requires, and exactly what Scepter wants built.

## The Recovery Problem (the side-quest spine)

The party can pull this thread into a real arc. Three leads, already seeded in `loose-ends.txt`:

1. **The prototype.** The ugly first field unit — the one that "ran too hot" — is the only device that can run the channel in **reverse**. It was signed out and never returned; trace it (a storage cage at a redacted site, a Scepter inventory, a black-market lab). Without it, nothing about restoration is even attemptable.
2. **The technician (the protester).** The honest witness who walked. He can be found by a party that asks the right way; he can explain what the records can't, and — at the default dial — he is the one hiding **P-1**. Winning his trust is the arc's social heart. He has spent years protecting a creature that sometimes wears the face of the woman his work destroyed.
3. **P-1 itself.** Find the Ditto-type that holds Aurora's self — possibly the very "Aurora" the party already met in Grandharbor — and you have found what Aurora lost. Find who holds it, and you have found your obstacle.

A complete restoration attempt wants all three: P-1 (where Aurora is), the prototype (the reverse channel), and the repair of Aurora's faculty (Amelia's research — which means dealing with Amelia, and with Scepter behind her).

## The Knife (the choice with no clean side)

If the party gets that far, hand them the dilemma the technology guarantees, and do not soften it. P-1 *is* the only place Aurora's complete self exists. It has spent years being her; at the high end of the dial it believes it is her; it can stand up and have a conversation as her, and the party may already have *had* that conversation in Grandharbor. Restoring the woman in the care bed means running the same door in reverse on P-1 — taking the self out of the creature that has been the living Aurora all this time, asking one Aurora to end so another can begin. And the blade has a second edge: the self in P-1 is whole and the body in the bed may be a husk that can never hold her again — so a thoughtful table will be forced to ask which one is even the *real* Aurora to save, and whether "her own body, empty" is worth unmaking "her whole self, in the wrong shape." There is no setting of the machine that answers it for them.

Meaningful outcomes, none of them tidy:
- **Full restoration**, at P-1's expense — the self is moved back into the mended body; the shapeshifter that *was* Aurora for years is emptied, or worse. Amelia gets her sister returned to her own face, and a fresh grief for the thing she destroyed to do it.
- **Partial transfer** — give the body back some of Aurora and leave P-1 diminished but extant; two half-sisters where there was one whole self in the wrong vessel — a half-cure and a half-mercy, the most "Shima" answer.
- **Refusal** — decide P-1's personhood is real and inviolable: Aurora already lives, just not where anyone wanted, and you will not kill the creature she became to refill the body she left. The woman in the bed stays empty; the party carries that choice. (Amelia will not forgive it.)
- **Leave her be** — judge that P-1, believing itself Aurora and living a life, *is* Aurora now, and let her keep it. The most radical mercy and the one Amelia can least bear.
- **The third way** — some attunement, ether, or Lorekeeper/Mystauran insight the campaign has seeded that lets experience be *shared* rather than *moved* — the door held open gently in both directions at once, the way the technology was always supposed to work and never did. Earn it; don't gift it.

## Campaign Connections

- **Amelia Pine** becomes fully legible: a villain whose every act traces to a sister and a flyer and a laugh about money. The desktop's `birthday-A-draft.txt` confession is the keystone — she is the architect of the wound she is killing herself to heal.
- **Team Scepter / the Scepter of Dominion.** Identity-displacement is dominion at its most intimate — the power to empty and overwrite a self. The experience-sharing research sits naturally in Scepter's portfolio of control, beside the artifacts and the shadow work.
- **The shadow door-work** (S.R.'s thread in `The_Pine_Cache.md`) rhymes with this on purpose: "a person has to be opened, and the opening does not close on its own." The Share and the shadow corruption are two studies of the same dreadful discovery — that a person can be opened, and not always closed. Amelia's clipped line in the cache, that the orb-work and the door-work "should not touch," reads differently once you know she has watched an opened door fail to close inside her own family.
- **The Exp. Share item** gains a haunted provenance. A party that learns this truth may never look at the cheerful little teaching device the same way — and that is the point.
- **Mystaura.** The tribes' *whakawhanaungatanga* and *mauri* describe the bond the Share crudely engineers; a Spirit Guide (*tohunga-hoa*) may be the one figure on the continent who understands experience-sharing as a sacred, reciprocal thing rather than a one-way extraction — a possible route to "the third way" above, and a reason for the arc to reach south.

## Reserved Dials (quick list)

- **P-1's holder** — the protester (default) / Amelia / Scepter / loose since Cache Thread 8.
- **Holding vs. becoming** — does P-1 merely hold Aurora, or believe it *is* her; sets the weight of The Knife and whether it knows what it is.
- **The Grandharbor meeting's purpose** — Amelia's field-test + surveillance (default) / P-1 loose and wandering / a Scepter op.
- **Scepter's timing** — inherited the research after (default) / funded it before.
- **Restorability** — full / partial only / a seeded third way; decide before the arc climaxes, not during.
