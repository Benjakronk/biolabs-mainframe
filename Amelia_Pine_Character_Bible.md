# Dr. Amelia Pine — Character Bible

**Companion to `What_Happened_To_Aurora.md`, `The_Pine_Cache.md`, and the Pine desktop minigame docs.**
*GM-facing. The locked canon is fixed; interpretation is my read for you to take or leave; reserved dials are flagged.*

> **Essence:** A grief-engine wearing a lab coat. A brilliant scientist whose entire moral architecture collapsed into one point — her sister — and who rebuilt herself around undoing a single mistake, no matter what she has to become, or build, or break, to do it. She is the rare antagonist who makes clean antagonism impossible: understand her and hating her gets hard, and *using* her gets complicated, because the party may end up holding the only things she wants.

---

## Locked Canon

- **Dr. Amelia Pine** — medical researcher, ether-paleontology and bond-science background; cover posting at **Starhaven Biolabs** (Libra University's institute); coordinating link to **Biolabs Site Alpha** (Project Chimera, the Veerulent pipeline). Embedded with **Team Scepter** — as an asset and a means, not a believer.
- **Aurora Pine is her younger sister** — the amnesiac at the center of everything (see `What_Happened_To_Aurora.md`).
- **She is the cause, not only the bereaved.** She read Aurora the trial flyer, called it harmless, laughed about the money. Her crusade is also penance.
- **Her Scepter membership is instrumental.** She joined to reach the experience-sharing research and the reverse-capable prototype — the only route to a cure — and discovered Scepter already owned it. They get a brilliant, motivated, conscience-compromised scientist; she gets access. Both sides believe they are using the other. Both are correct.
- **Current location: Blackthorn Manor.** Installed in the old observatory tower study (Nero's room when he is present), a semi-captive asset in the inner sanctum — deeper access, narrower freedom, watched even on her own account (the desktop's co-user).
- **Signature partner: a Testurion** (Falinks line, Fighting/Steel, #168) that **may evolve to Gladirena** (#169). See *Her Pokémon*, below.
- **The "Aurora" the party met in Grandharbor was P-1** — Amelia's Project Proteus shapeshifter (Ditto-type), which is the *same creature* that holds Aurora's displaced self (the carrier and P-1 are one). See *The Grandharbor "Aurora"*, below.
- **Endgame, deferred:** after going dark (Pine Cache Thread 8) she eventually resurfaces under the pseudonym **"Aurora Drake"** — her lost sister's name, borrowed — authoring desert-crisis propaganda. (Far-future; not needed yet.)

## Character Interpretation

**The catastrophe is the whole engine.** Everything Amelia does now runs on one event and one debt. She has no appetite for Nero's revolution, the Scepter of Dominion, or any cause larger than a hospital room. Strip the ideology away and what remains is a woman doing arithmetic that always comes out the same: *this gets me closer to fixing Aurora, therefore I do it.* The Veerulent coordination, the Chimera work, the surveillance of her own students through Yucca's innocence, the grooming of Maxine Blaze, the cold appraisal of the party as "the surveyors we cannot replace" — all of it is downstream of a cure.

**Penance has no stopping point.** Because she caused the wound, her crusade doubles as atonement, and atonement against an infinite debt sets no natural limit. Relative to what she did, nothing reads as *too far*. That is her engine and her damnation in a single mechanism: she will keep walking toward the cure off any cliff, because stopping means abandoning Aurora, and she forfeited the right to stop the day she read out the flyer.

**She knows exactly what she is.** This is the most human and most playable thing about her. The Pine Cache shows the competence and the ruthlessness; the desktop shows the woman drowning. The resignation letter revised forty times and never sent is the entire character in one object — clarity enough to condemn herself, never quite enough to quit. She sees the cliff, narrates the fall, and keeps walking. Do not let her be unaware. Let her be *unable to stop*, which is worse and far more affecting.

**Her self-justification is true, and that is the trap.** "I am using them, not serving them" may be accurate — and it does not matter, because the work does not care about reasons. A weapon built by a grieving sister kills the same as one built by a zealot, and the cure she is chasing *is* the weapon Scepter wants: the machine that saves Aurora and the machine that empties and overwrites a human mind are one machine. She is too smart not to know this and too committed to let it stop her. She has simply filed the contradiction, the way she files everything.

**The control is a dam, not a cure.** She compartmentalizes ferociously — "P-1 in every document, not its species," results that don't leave the bay, threads kept from Bellini. But the compartments leak, and the leaks are where the person lives: she plays Aurora's songs in the cold tower, keeps the photographs as her wallpaper, cannot finish the confession in the trash. Her over-control is itself the tell. The dam holds the grief back; it does not drain it.

**Compressed name note:** *Pine* carries both the longing and the evergreen — grief and constancy in one word; *Amelia* means industrious. An industrious longing. It fits her better than it probably needs to.

## The Grandharbor "Aurora" — P-1 Is the Carrier

**The locked fact:** the lucid, conversational Aurora Pine the party met in Grandharbor was **P-1**, Amelia's Proteus shapeshifter (Ditto-type) — and P-1 is the **same creature** that holds Aurora's stolen self. Carrier and Proteus asset are one animal (see `What_Happened_To_Aurora.md`). The trial wired Aurora to a Ditto-type *because* a formless shapeshifter is the most receptive vessel; under the overloaded channel it became her, and a shapeshifter can then *wear* what it holds. The party did not meet a fake or a reconstruction. They met the truest surviving Aurora there is — her whole, pre-loss self, standing up in her own shape, because the thing holding her can be her on demand.

This reconciles the contradiction the table noticed: the *living* Aurora is amnesiac, gentle, and empty in care and could not have had those conversations; the Grandharbor Aurora was vivid and articulate because she was P-1 running Aurora's intact self.

**Why it tightens the cache.** "P-1 in every document, not its species" now reads as Amelia hiding that the asset *is her sister* — exposing it exposes her motive and her one leverage point. Galceran's "held a transformed state past twenty-four hours / replication applications" is P-1 holding Aurora's form indefinitely — the result that obsesses her. "Do not let the duration result reach Bellini, especially not there" becomes the most dangerous secret she owns: P-1 *being Aurora.* The coldness of those memos is the dam.

**What it does to Amelia.** Her project is not "reconstruct my sister" — the miracle already happened, uninvited, and it is the wrong shape. Aurora is *alive*, whole, in a Pokémon's body, while the woman in the care bed stays empty. Amelia is trapped between two sisters and cannot have both: the self in P-1 is perfect; the body in the bed may be a husk that can never hold her again. Her work is now extraction and restoration — get Aurora *out* and *back where she belongs* — and the horror is that she may have to unmake the living Aurora to refill the empty one, and she cannot choose, and she cannot stop trying.

**Why it was in Grandharbor (dials):**
- *Field test + surveillance (default):* Amelia sent P-1-as-Aurora to move among the party — the surveyors she values — gauging them up close while watching her sister talk to the world again. Espionage and grief in one act.
- *Loose since Thread 8:* post–going-dark, P-1 is off the leash, wearing the only shape it trusts, perhaps believing it *is* Aurora — a recurring, uncanny NPC the party keeps crossing.
- *Scepter op:* a handler used the asset to steer the party. Coldest, least tragic, available if you want a thriller beat.

**Payoffs to bank:**
- **The curdle.** When the party learns the woman in care is the empty one, every Grandharbor memory inverts — *who did we talk to?* — and the mismatch between the two Auroras is the clue that cracks Project Proteus, the Share, and Amelia's whole project at once.
- **The recurring uncanny.** A P-1 that may not fully know what it is — that answers to Aurora's name because it half-believes the name — is one of the best long-term NPCs available to you: pathos and horror in one body, and a living argument about what a person is.
- **The sharpened Knife.** Because P-1 is the *only* place Aurora's whole self exists, restoring the body in care means ending the Aurora who already walks and talks. One blade, two edges; see the Aurora doc.

## Her Pokémon

**Testurion → Gladirena (Falinks line, Fighting/Steel) — her chosen partner, and a stat-block portrait of her.**

The line reads as Amelia almost too well, and you can lean on it:

- **Battle Armor** (immune to extra damage from crits): nothing can land the sudden, unseen blow that breaks her — because the only critical hit that mattered already landed, years ago, and she armored over the crater. She cannot be caught off guard by grief again; she lives inside it.
- **Defiant** (afflict it, and its next attack has advantage): pressure her, corner her, lay a condition on her, and she answers harder. She does not fold under affliction; affliction is her native climate.
- **Formation Leader** (Gladirena): commands a formation whose members' movement is *held until the leader moves them.* This is Amelia's whole method — she moves the party, Maxine, Yucca, her staff like ranks in a formation only she can see, each piece holding position until she advances it. Her tragedy in the ability's fine print: a formation-commander with no formation that knows it's in formation, and none she trusts.
- **The evolution as a story metronome.** Testurion evolves to Gladirena by **winning a gladiatorial dominance tournament** — evolution earned by *prevailing*. Use the timing deliberately. Her Testurion still being a Testurion says she has not yet won the thing she's fighting for; the moment it becomes **Gladirena** can mark her crossing some threshold — the disciplined legionnaire of grief hardening into the commander who will move the whole field to get what she wants. Or withhold the evolution forever as quiet characterization: she endures, she holds the line, she never triumphs, and the partner stays the soldier that mirrors her.

The disciplined, shelled, patient Fighting/Steel soldier suits her temperament far better than any showy ace would. She does not battle to dazzle. She holds, armors, endures, and advances on her own count.

**P-1 (Proteus / Ditto-type)** is the other half of her "team" and emphatically not a battler — her most guarded asset, her surveillance instrument, and the creature that holds her sister's stolen self (it *is* the carrier; see above). Treat it as a plot entity, not a combatant. If it ever fights, it fights wearing someone else's shape — often Aurora's — which is its own horror.

## Relationship to the Party

- **The antagonist who breaks clean antagonism.** Early, she is a name behind betrayals — the operative who turned their own professor into an unwitting informant, who values them as tools. The instant they understand *why*, the moral picture muddies: she is a villain whose every act traces to a sister and a flyer and a laugh. A paladin will hesitate. That hesitation is the design.
- **The leverage knot.** Pursue the Aurora arc and the party may come to hold the exact things Amelia needs — the carrier, the prototype, the technician, the route to the cure. That makes them simultaneously her enemies, her potential saviors, and her marks. One arc can be all three.
- **The mirror.** Amelia is what the party *becomes* if they decide at the Knife that one person's restoration justifies any cost. Running her well means letting the table watch their own worst option walk around in a body, being reasonable.

## Redemption Axis & Endpoints

Her redeemability runs through **Aurora and nothing else** — appeals to conscience bounce off a woman who condemned herself years ago and kept going; the only lever that moves her is the cure, which means the party holding it is the only thing that can turn her. (*Whether she is redeemable at all is a reserved dial.*) Natural endpoints:

- **The cure, at a cost** — she gets Aurora back at the carrier's or P-1's expense: a restoration and a fresh horror, and a sister who may not thank her.
- **Stopped short** — the party or events end her project before it's finished; Aurora stays gone; Amelia shatters, or hollows into something colder.
- **The third way** — pulled into helping find the *shared-not-moved* solution (possibly via a Mystauran Spirit Guide); the gentlest and hardest ending, and the only one that might let her forgive herself.
- **The unfinished letter** — she dies still revising the resignation she never sent, the debt never paid, the dam finally overtopped.

## Running Her — Voice & Tells

- **Register:** dry, precise, controlled — a scientist's economy. She discusses atrocity and logistics in the same flat tone, which unsettles more than any rant.
- **She files her villainy; she does not monologue it.** No gloating, no speeches. She states costs and trade-offs as though reading a method section. Let the *reasonableness* be the horror.
- **Never let her plead.** Self-abasement isn't in her. She owns what she's done without flinching and without asking forgiveness; the accountability without the apology is chilling on its own.
- **The leaks are the tells.** A half-second pause. A deflection when the conversation drifts toward Aurora. The over-control tightening. She will change the subject from her sister with surgical speed — and a party that notices *that* has found the seam.
- **Tactically (in a battle):** patient and defensive, like her Testurion — armor up, endure, punish overreach, advance on her own count. She does not chase. She makes you come to her and regret it.
- **One line that is her whole self,** for when you need it: *"I know what I am. I worked it out a long time ago. It changes nothing — I still have a sister, and she still doesn't have me."*

## Reserved Dials (quick list)

- **Does P-1 know what it is** — believes it *is* Aurora (pure tragedy) / knows it is a Pokémon holding her (stranger, sadder). Sets the weight of every scene it's in.
- **P-1's status now** — Amelia's controlled asset / loose since Cache Thread 8 / Scepter-held / with the protester.
- **What the Grandharbor meeting was for** — field-test + surveillance (default) / loose-and-wandering / Scepter op.
- **Testurion's evolution** — withheld as characterization / triggered as a threshold beat (decide what "winning" means for her first).
- **Redeemability** — turnable via the cure / past saving / genuinely undecided until the table forces it.
- **Protective feeling toward the party** — does the woman who values them as "surveyors" come to feel anything more? Your call; powerful either way.
