# PINE.OS — CONTENT PACK
### Finished, paste-ready file contents for the desktop minigame
**Companion to `Pine_Desktop_DesignDoc.md`.** Every file the design doc describes is written out below as final prose. The builder can drop these straight into the filesystem objects. Each entry gives: the in-OS **filename**, the **app** that opens it, a one-line **purpose tag**, and the **body**.

**Conventions (consistent with `The_Pine_Cache.md`):** personal mail = `amelia.pine@home.net`; work = `a.pine@starhaven.lib`; secured = `████.sec`. Redactions render as `████`. "E." = Elias/Nero, "H." = Helena Blackthorn — never spelled out in-fiction. Current year for timestamps: **848 AFR**; use the calendar's common month names if you want dated files (placeholders below use plain dates the GM can restyle).

**Camouflage ratio reminder:** plot files are marked ⭑ THREAD A/B/C. Mundane files are marked ○. Keep roughly 2–3 ○ per ⭑ visible in any open folder.

```
They've given me the old observatory at the top of the tower to work in.
Five flights of cold stone and a door that doesn't lock from the inside.
The lenses are gone — sold, like everything here — but the mounts are still
bolted to the floor, pointing at nothing. Someone pinned star charts where the
sky used to be.

It's E.'s room when he's here. His maps are still on the curved wall. I move my
papers around them and try not to read them and read them anyway. I try not to
be here when he is.

The window faces the sea. On clear mornings you can watch the weather come in

## ~/Files/Aurora/  (THREAD B — the sister, the amnesia, the investigation)

### ⭑ `she-asked-again.txt` — Notes app (grief journal style) — *the emotional core*
```
She asked who I was again today.

I told her I was her sister. She smiled — that easy smile she still has, the one
thing it didn't take — and she said, "I always wanted a sister." Like it was good
news. Like I was a stranger bringing her a gift.

I said yes. You have one. I'm right here.

She asked if I'd visit again. As if I am someone who visits. As if I have ever,
once, since this started, gone more than nine days without standing in that room.

I left before she saw my face do the thing it does.

I am going to fix this. I do not care what it costs or who I have to become to buy
the means. I have stopped pretending that sentence frightens me.
```

### ⭑ `timeline.txt` — Notes app — *the methodical hunt; plants the trial*
```
AURORA — LAST LUCID MONTHS (reconstructed, best effort)

— Spring: normal. Letters, visits, the river survey work. Sharp as ever.
— Early summer: the trial. She volunteered. "Paid, harmless, twenty minutes with
  a partner Pokémon and a very polite technician." Her words, in her last normal
  letter. I have read that letter four hundred times.
— After: three weeks of "tired." Headaches. Names going soft at the edges.
— Then the drop. Not gradual after that point. A door closing.
— By autumn she did not reliably know our mother. By winter she did not know me.

The doctors call it idiopathic. Idiopathic means "we don't know and we've stopped
looking." I have not stopped looking.

The ONLY anomaly in her year is the trial. Everyone wants me to accept coincidence.
I am a scientist. I do not believe in a healthy woman whose mind empties three weeks
after she was wired to a Pokémon and told to "share an experience."
```

### ⭑ `records-wall.txt` — Notes app — *hitting walls; why she went to Scepter*
```
What I have been able to get:
  - Her intake form. Real. Her signature. The program name redacted on the copy
    they released, of course.
  - A consent waiver so broad it consents to the heat death of the universe.
  - Nothing else. The trial's sponsor "cannot locate" the dataset. The technician
    "is no longer with the program." The program "concluded."

What I have learned trying:
  - A legitimate request gets you a legitimate wall.
  - The people who ran that trial have resources, lawyers, and silence I cannot
    out-spend or out-wait.
  - There is exactly one kind of organisation that can open doors like these, and
    they came to ME, in the end. They offered access. I have stopped asking myself
    whether I should have said no. The question is a luxury and Aurora does not have
    time for my conscience.

If the share took something out of her, the share can be made to give it back.
First I have to find where it went.
```

## ~/Files/Research/shared-exp/  (THREAD C — the Exp. Share origin & side-quest seeds)

### ⭑ `share-principle.txt` — Notes app — *the core concept; Exp. Share lore*
```
PRINCIPLE OF THE SHARE (working notes — mine, not theirs)

A trainer and a bonded Pokémon already exchange more than commands. The bond
carries feeling, intent, a thread of shared life — the southern tribes have a word,
mauri, and they are not wrong to use it. The Share is the engineering of that thread.

Done lightly, it is a teaching tool. A partner that did not stand in the fight still
receives the *experience* of it along the link — the lesson without the danger. Hang
the device between trainer and a second partner and the second partner grows from
battles it never entered. That is the version they want to sell. That is the version
that is safe.

That is not the version that interests me, and it is not, I am now certain, the
version Aurora was given.
```

### ⭑ `door-both-ways.txt` — Notes app — *the dual edge; the gut-punch link to Aurora*
```
The Share is not a teaching tool. It is a door. Doors swing both ways.

Experience can flow to a partner. It can also flow FROM a person — and the link does
not care which direction is "supposed." Push the gain too hard, hold the channel open
too long, and you are not copying experience anymore. You are MOVING it. And what moves
out does not always move back.

Memory is experience that stayed. I can no longer pretend I don't know what that means
for a healthy woman who sat in a chair for twenty minutes, wired to a partner, told to
open the channel as wide as it would go "for a clean reading."

Somewhere there is a Pokémon carrying twenty minutes of my sister that she will never
get back unless I take it out and put it where it belongs. If that is even possible.
I will make it possible.
```

### ⭑ `loose-ends.txt` — Notes app — *explicit side-quest hooks*
```
THREADS I CAN STILL PULL:

1. The prototype. The early field unit — not the polished demo, the ugly first one
   that ran too hot — was never logged back into inventory. Last I can place it: a
   storage cage at ████████, signed out and never returned. If anyone still has a unit
   that can run the channel in REVERSE, it is that one.

2. The technician. "No longer with the program" — because he WALKED. He saw something
   in an early session that frightened him out of a career. I have a name and a region.
   A man who quit in protest will talk, if the right person asks the right way. He may
   be the only honest witness left.

3. The carrier. If a subject's experience went somewhere, it went into a PARTNER. Find
   the partner Pokémon from Aurora's trial and you find what she lost — "still carrying
   something that isn't its own," if the early notes are right. It was never returned to
   the wild or to records. Someone kept it. Find who.

Three threads. Any one of them is a door. I have stopped being afraid of doors.
```
